2.6. Intelligent Metasurfaces Experiment with Coupling
Involvement
To further verify the performance of GNN in real scenarios,
we have fabricated an intelligent metasurface comprising of
20 × 20-unit cells. The intelligent metasurface system consists
of the metasurface, control circuits, ﬁeld programmable gate ar-
ray (FPGA), and computer (Supplementary Note S5, Support-
ing Information). The FPGA can output serial signals of high
and low voltage levels through programming and then the con-
trol circuit is responsible for converting them into parallel for-
mat. The metasurface is processed using conventional printed
circuit board (PCB) techniques, with the substrate material being
F4B (the dielectric constant of 2.65). Each unit cell of metasur-
face is equipped with an SMP1320 diode, which can be indepen-
dently voltage-controlled through the control circuit. The diode
exhibits diﬀerent equivalent capacitances when it is in “on” and
“oﬀ” stages, which enables signiﬁcant variations in the reﬂection
coeﬃcient of the unit cell at the target frequency.
We have conducted the experiment in a microwave anechoic
chamber, and Figure 5c shows the near-ﬁeld measurement en-
vironment. The near-ﬁeld information of the metasurface at 5.9
GHz is collected using the near-ﬁeld scanning system, and the
scanning plane is half a wavelength away from the metasurface.
We collected the data by changing the reﬂection response of the
metasurface using FPGA and control circuits. After the GNN is
trained, we have utilized it to predict the near-ﬁeld of the metasur-
face and the results are shown in Figure 5d. We can see that the
near-ﬁelds predicted by GNN closely align with the experimental
results, demonstrating the eﬀectiveness of GNN in practical ap-
plications. The slight discrepancies between GNN prediction re-
sults and experimental results may be caused by the interference
of background waves and the fabrication errors of the intelligent
metasurface.
