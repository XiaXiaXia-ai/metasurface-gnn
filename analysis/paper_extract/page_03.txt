To fully unearth the hidden patterns within the data, a large
amount of data is required for the training of GNN. Typically,
the simulation data pertaining to each metasurface array is re-
garded as an integrated entity, rendering it inseparable. But for
large arrays, this practice makes it too time-consuming and even
impractical to collect enough data to train the neural network.
To address this problem, we employ the sliding window method
that eﬀectively partitions the data of large arrays for eﬃcient pro-
cessing. As illustrated in Figure 2c, we use a 5 × 5 red window to
slide over the metasurface, sampling the features of unit cells and
near-ﬁeld at each step, resulting in a set of 5 × 5 features of unit
cells and their corresponding near-ﬁeld. The window size is set
as 5 × 5 so that all the unit cells that can inﬂuence the central unit
cells can be included, as we consider the coupling within the dis-
tance of two-unit cells. Furthermore, since the unit cells outside
the window also have mutual coupling eﬀects on the unit cells in-
side, whereas the sampling process does not collect information
from the unit cells outside the window, the network may overlook
these coupling eﬀects. To avoid neglecting the inﬂuence of unit
cells outside the window on the inside unit cells, we use a 9 × 9
blue window to sample the features of unit cells and only sample
the near ﬁeld within the central 5 × 5 red window. The sliding
window method allows us to obtain suﬃcient data by simulating
only a small number of arrays.
2.2. Architecture of GNN
The performance of GNN hinges on their well-crafted architec-
tures, particularly the pivotal graph convolution layer, which is
responsible for eﬃciently processing graph-structured data and
extracting intricate features. While stacking multiple layers of
graph convolution layers typically improves the performance of
the network, it also makes the model more complex and increases
the diﬃculty of training. Therefore, achieving better performance
with fewer network parameters is one of the standard for evalu-
ating the eﬀectiveness of GNN.[37,38]
