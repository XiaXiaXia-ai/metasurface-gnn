General Characterization of Intelligent Metasurfaces with
Graph Coupling Network
Ouling Wu, Chao Qian,* Zhixiang Fan,* Xiaoyue Zhu, and Hongsheng Chen*
Intelligent metasurfaces, as the next-generation of metasurfaces, have
emerged as a versatile artiﬁcial electromagnetic (EM) medium capable of
adaptively manipulating wave-matter interactions, especially in the
construction of EM space integration and the analogy of wave-based neural
networks. However, current computational landscape for intelligent
metasurfaces relies either on time-consuming full-wave numerical
simulations with excessive computational complexity or on
application-limited physical models that are diﬃcult to consider the coupling
eﬀects. Here, a universal graph neural network (GNN) framework is
introduced, incorporating the elusive coupling eﬀects inside metasurfaces,
enabling rapid and precise characterization with arbitrary-large size. This
framework exhibits exceptional compatibility with physical models, thereby
signiﬁcantly expanding the realm of potential design scenarios. By harnessing
the principles of diﬀraction theory and near-to-far transformation algorithms,
highly accurate modeling of the scattered ﬁelds emanating from metasurfaces
is achieved. Through microwave experiments on intelligent metasurfaces, the
eﬃcacy of GNN in real-world scenarios is eﬀectively demonstrated. The
utilization of topological strategies to characterize intelligent metasurfaces
marks a major leap toward the next-generation metasurfaces, promising
transformative advancements across various applications.

Due to the critical importance of precision and reliability for
intelligent metasurfaces, the coupling eﬀects arising from their
physical structures cannot be ignored, especially for large-scale
or strongly coupled arrays.[27] Nevertheless, it poses a formidable
challenge to accurately characterize and understand metasur-
faces coupling mechanisms, particularly in high-sensitivity re-
quired applications like stealth and radar systems. Most theoret-
ical methods, such as antenna theory and geometrical optics, are
based on the assumption that each unit is independent, neglect-
ing coupling among them. While traditional deep neural net-
works can implicitly incorporate the coupling eﬀects, their per-
formances are often constrained by the characteristics of training
data due to the lack of physical guidance, leading to limited gener-
alization and adaptability to the diverse physical properties inher-
ent in metasurface design.[28,29] All these obstacles boil down to
a high demand for accurately characterizing intelligent metasur-
