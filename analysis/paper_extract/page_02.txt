In this work, we construct a lightweight and powerful GNN to
accurately characterize arbitrary-large intelligent metasurfaces by
involving the coupling eﬀects among unit cells (Figure 1). Based
on the basic near-ﬁeld prediction at a given position, the scattered
ﬁeld at any distance and far-ﬁeld pattern of a metasurface can be
easily derived by combining the output of GNN with diﬀraction
theory and near-to-far transformation algorithms. We train the
network using full-wave simulation data and validate the reliabil-
ity in near-ﬁeld prediction. Besides, GNN can also be used for
inverse design. By connecting GNN with gradient descent algo-
rithm, we have developed an inverse design pipeline for near-
ﬁeld hologram imaging and utilized it to manipulate the meta-
surface to generate a triangular-shaped near-ﬁeld. As an experi-
mental veriﬁcation, we have designed and fabricated an intelli-
gent metasurface to validate the eﬀects of GNN in real-world sce-
narios. The alignment between experimental and predicted near-
ﬁelds demonstrates the validity of GNN in practical applications.
Our work opens a new avenue for accurately characterizing intel-
ligent metasurfaces and makes a big step for the next-generation
intelligent metasurfaces and associated applications.
2. Results
2.1. Characterizing Coupling Eﬀects with GNN
As a data-driven method, GNN necessitates original data that en-
capsulates coupling eﬀects, whereas full-wave simulations oﬀer
the capability to numerically compute the intricate coupling in-
teractions among unit cells. In the modeling process of metasur-
faces, we establish a reﬂective metasurface model in Computer
Simulation Technology (CST) Microwave Studio with 30 × 30-
unit cells. As shown in Figure 2a, each unit cell has a width of
14 mm, and the substrate material is FR4 with a thickness of
1.5 mm. By adjusting the radius of top round metal patch from
3.0 to 4.0 mm, the reﬂected phase almost covers 0°–360°, while
the reﬂected amplitude remains high at the target frequency of
10 GHz. After setting the incident wave as an x-polarized plane
wave, we conduct the full-wave simulation and subsequently
gather the electric ﬁeld (E-ﬁeld) data on the plane positioned at
half a wavelength away from the metasurface.
Due to the mutual coupling among unit cells, the near ﬁeld
above each unit cell is not only determined by itself, but also
aﬀected by the surrounding unit cells. As one of the most ad-
vanced techniques for addressing deep learning problems in
non-Euclidean space, GNN can eﬀectively utilize the topological
characteristics of graph-structured data to compute relational in-
formation between nodes, which allows for the quantitative cal-
culation of the coupling eﬀects of surrounding unit cells on the
central unit cells, enabling precise predictions of its near-ﬁeld. In
