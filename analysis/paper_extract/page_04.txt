Here, we propose a lightweight GNN framework that consists
of only one graph convolution layer and six 2D convolution lay-
ers, as shown in Figure 3b. The graph convolution layer adopts
the idea of message aggregation, where the feature of each node
is sent as a message to its neighboring nodes, and node features
are updated accordingly (Supplementary Note S2, Supporting In-
formation). The feature updating process is as follows. Assume
node j is a neighbor of node i and their features are denoted as
nj and ni respectively. First, each node sends its own features as
messages to its neighboring nodes. Then the message nj received
by node i is concatenated with its own feature ni and the feature
of edge between them, which is denoted as eij. After that, they un-
dergo a transformation by the reduce function f1, becoming the
coupling eﬀect cij of node j on node i, as Equation (1) shows.

In the architecture of GNN, both functions f1 and f2 consist of

After training, the graph convolution layer will be able to pre-
dict the near ﬁeld corresponding to each unit cell according to
their new features. To obtain the entire near-ﬁeld within the 5 × 5
window, we can concatenate the near-ﬁelds of each unit cell.
However, this concatenation may induce noticeable discontinu-
ities at the junctions of the near ﬁelds. To mitigate this issue, we
add six layers of 2D convolution layers after the graph convolu-
tion layer, which act as ﬁlters to smooth the near ﬁelds at the
junctions. Due to the strategy of concatenating the near-ﬁelds of
each unit cell, even though we only use unit cells and near-ﬁelds
within the window as training data, the GNN is able to predict
the near-ﬁelds of metasurfaces with arbitrary-large size. To em-
phasize this point, we have randomly generated metasurfaces of
diﬀerent sizes and utilized GNN to predict their near-ﬁelds, the
corresponding results are shown in Figure 3d,e. For 5 × 5 and
30 × 30 metasurfaces, the Pearson correlation coeﬃcients be-
tween the prediction and simulation results are 0.938 and 0.870,
respectively. It can be observed that, due to the incorporation of
coupling information, GNN achieves highly accurate results in
metasurfaces with diﬀerent sizes.
