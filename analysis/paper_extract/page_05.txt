to compute the scattered ﬁelds of highly coupled metasurfaces
may induce substantial inaccuracies.
Despite the mentioned drawbacks of diﬀraction method, it can
accurately calculate the scattered ﬁelds at any distance for known
near-ﬁelds. This compensates for the limitation of GNN that it
can only predict the near-ﬁeld at a ﬁxed distance. By combining
GNN with diﬀraction method, we can accurately obtain the scat-
tered ﬁelds of metasurfaces with arbitrary-large size at any dis-
tance. We have randomly generated a 30 × 30 metasurface and
employed diﬀerent methods to calculate the scattered ﬁelds on
the planes located 20 and 30 wavelengths away from it. The per-
formance is shown in Figure 4b,c. The scattered ﬁelds computed
using GNN closely align with the simulation results, showcasing
minimal deviation, whereas the outcomes derived solely from the
diﬀraction method manifest notable distortions.
2.5. Near-to-Far Transformation
In numerous metasurfaces applications, particularly in the realm
of wireless communications, the far-ﬁeld behavior holds signiﬁ-
cant importance. Although the GNN is only used to predict the
near-ﬁelds of metasurfaces in this work, the far-ﬁelds can be eas-
ily obtained by near-to-far transformation algorithms,[43,44] fur-
ther expanding its application scenarios. Here we have employed
the plane wave expansion method for far-ﬁeld calculation.[45] The
near-ﬁeld of a metasurface can be written as a superposition of
plane waves (all of the same frequency, diﬀerent amplitudes, and
traveling in diﬀerent directions) of the form f (kx, ky) exp(−jkr).
The function f (kx, ky) is the vector amplitude of the wave, and kx
and ky are the spectral frequencies which extend over the entire
frequency spectrum. Thus, the E-ﬁeld in spherical coordinates
